# Mission Statement

## Purpose

TaylorDo exists to improve the usefulness, performance, reliability, and operational lifetime of mechanical hard disk drive systems through sound engineering, thoughtful storage organization, and automated preventive maintenance.

The project is based on the belief that mature technologies continue to deserve careful engineering while they remain in widespread use.

## Our Mission

Millions of people throughout the world continue to rely on computers equipped with mechanical hard disk drives.

For many individuals, schools, businesses, community organizations, and public institutions, replacing existing hardware is neither economically practical nor environmentally desirable.

TaylorDo seeks to help these users obtain the maximum practical value from the systems they already own.

Its objective is to restore lost performance where possible, maintain long-term storage organization, and encourage preventive maintenance that helps systems remain useful for longer.

## Engineering Philosophy

TaylorDo treats storage optimization as a systems engineering problem.

The project emphasizes:

- Workload-oriented storage organization.
- Intelligent physical placement of data.
- Preventive rather than corrective maintenance.
- Repeatable and deterministic operation.
- Safe optimization practices appropriate to the underlying storage technology.
- Open documentation of the engineering principles behind every design decision.

## Scientific Purpose

TaylorDo also serves as an exploration of storage architecture and software engineering.

The project demonstrates that:

- Storage organization extends beyond simple defragmentation.
- Physical data placement continues to influence HDD performance.
- Sophisticated architectures can be implemented using constrained domain-specific languages.
- Engineering principles remain applicable to mature technologies.

## Sustainability

The project recognizes that extending the useful life of existing computer systems can provide practical benefits.

Where appropriate, improved storage organization and preventive maintenance may:

- Delay unnecessary hardware replacement.
- Reduce maintenance effort.
- Improve the usefulness of existing systems.
- Encourage responsible use of computing resources.

Across large numbers of deployed systems, even modest improvements in longevity and efficiency may contribute to reduced electronic waste and more sustainable use of existing hardware.

## Global Perspective

TaylorDo is intended to benefit users wherever mechanical hard disk drives remain in active service.

This includes homes, educational institutions, small businesses, governments, non-profit organizations, and communities where access to newer computing hardware may be limited.

The project seeks to make effective storage optimization available without regard to geography or economic circumstance.

## Open Collaboration

TaylorDo is developed as a free and open source project.

The project is intended to encourage collaboration among users, educators, researchers, software developers, and organizations interested in storage optimization and systems engineering.

The software, documentation, and Design Science materials are published under a commercially friendly open source license that encourages both academic and practical use.

The objective is to remove unnecessary barriers to adoption while ensuring that the ideas, engineering principles, and implementation remain openly available for study, improvement, and responsible application.

## Vision

TaylorDo demonstrates that good engineering continues to matter long after a technology reaches maturity.

As long as mechanical hard disk drives remain in productive use, opportunities exist to improve their organization, maintainability, efficiency, and operational lifetime through disciplined engineering.

Modern hard disk drives remain mechanical devices.

Their intelligence has increased substantially.

Their physics have not.
